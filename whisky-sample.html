SAMPLE — JAPANESE WHISKY

Rare whisky, two prices for one bottle

Closed distilleries and limited releases trade at two very different prices: what a bottle costs in Japan, and what the same bottle hammers for at an overseas auction. This sample shows four bottles where both sides could be confirmed from public sources on Nov 24, 2026. FX: ¥158.70 / $1, ¥208 / £1. No sale or brokerage of alcohol — this page is a data sample only.

Net after fees — the fee schedule (confirmed Nov 24, 2026)

  • Whisky Auctioneer buyer’s commission: 15% of the hammer + 20% VAT on that commission = +18% on the hammer price (fee schedule as of Nov 1, 2026; the old 12.5% rate was raised to 15% on Jul 1, 2026).
  • Whisky Auctioneer seller’s commission: 5% (reference value; it bites the seller, not the buyer).
  • Sotheby’s / Bonhams: buyer’s premium varies by house and lot, so no single rate is fixed here (e.g. Sworders charges 20% for wine & spirits from Jul 1, 2026). The Yamazaki 55 hammer below is shown gross only for that reason.
  • Yamazaki 25 — net: the hammer was already −¥108,125 before fees. A buyer’s real cost is $6,250 × 1.18 = $7,375 (¥1,170,413), higher still. No arbitrage, confirmed.
  • Yamazaki 55 — net: the ¥3,000,000 release price is not buyable today, so there is no trade to net against the $200,000 hammer. The +958% is a premium over a past release price, not a margin.
  • Hibiki 30 — net: the hammer was already −¥206,000 before fees. A buyer’s real cost is £2,500 × 1.18 = £2,950 (¥613,600), still below the Japan shop price of ¥726,000. No arbitrage in either direction — the Japan shop is simply the expensive side.
  • Hakushu 25 — net: the hammer was already −¥136,835 before fees. A buyer’s real cost is $2,950 × 1.18 = $3,481 (¥552,435), still below the Japan shop price of ¥605,000. No arbitrage, confirmed.
  • Conclusion: after the fee schedule is applied, all four sample rows remain “no buyable arbitrage”. The next value in this dataset is completing the one-sided prices of the remaining six bottles.

HOW WE COMPARE

Same bottle, same release — or no row

Whisky prices swing with release year, cask and condition, so a comparison is only valid when the bottle matches exactly.

  • Bottle: distillery + age statement + release year + cask type must match exactly. A different vintage of the same age statement is a different bottle.
  • Japan side: shop retail first; maker’s suggested retail shown only with the note that it is lottery-only. Buyback quotes are labelled “buyback” and never mixed with retail.
  • Overseas side: hammer price at a named auction house and sale date. Buyer’s premium is excluded from the hammer and stated as a cost to be added, not hidden.
  • Sources: public retail listings and published auction results only. No member-only auction data, no scraped private marketplaces. We do not sell or broker whisky; this page is a data sample only.
